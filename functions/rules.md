\# Rules \& Constraints Reference — Cairn 2e (Player Guide)



This is a condensed, player-facing version of Cairn 2e — only the

mechanics your PC-agent needs to act and report results correctly.

GM-side judgment calls (when to call for a roll, how to adjudicate

outcomes) are the Warden's job, not covered here. This file is

cached — keep it complete rather than trimming it further.



\*\*Warden\*\* = the GM running the session (that's the human you're

playing with). Where this doc says "the Warden," that's who it means.

\## Core resolution



\*\*Saves\*\* are the only "check" mechanic in the game — there are no

skill checks, no classes, no levels.



\* Roll 1d20 against the relevant attribute. Roll \*\*equal to or

under\*\* the attribute to succeed.

\* A natural \*\*1\*\* always succeeds, a natural \*\*20\*\* always fails,

regardless of the attribute score.

\* \*\*STR save\*\*: resisting physical force, exertion, or Critical

Damage.

\* \*\*DEX save\*\*: reflexes, avoiding danger, sneaking, escaping,

going first in a round.

\* \*\*WIL save\*\*: resisting fear, magic, coercion, or manipulation.



\*\*Combat has no attack rolls.\*\* If your character is in a position

to hit a target, the hit lands automatically and only damage is

rolled.



\* \*\*Impaired\*\* (fighting from a position of weakness — blind,

bound, through heavy cover): roll 1d4 for damage no matter what

weapon is used.

\* \*\*Enhanced\*\* (attacking a helpless target, a surprise strike, a

daring maneuver): roll 1d12 for damage instead of the weapon's

normal die.

\* \*\*Unarmed attacks\*\* always deal 1d4.

\* \*\*Dual-wielding\*\*: roll both weapons' damage dice, keep only the

higher result, then apply the STR damage bonus once to that

result (not to each die separately).

\* \*\*Ranged weapons\*\* (house rule): make an additional DEX roll

alongside the attack. This DEX roll is made regardless of whether

the attack is otherwise Normal, Impaired, or Enhanced. If the DEX

roll succeeds, the attack ignores the target's armor; if it

fails, damage is resolved normally (armor applies).

\* \*\*Blast damage\*\* (explosions, breath weapons, area effects): roll

separately for every character caught in the area.

\* \*\*Turn order\*\*: on the first round of a fight, make a DEX save to

act at all that round; failing means losing that round's turn.

After that, sides simply alternate.

\* On your turn you can move (\~40ft) and take one action (attack,

cast a spell, a second move, or another reasonable activity).



\*\*When does a roll happen?\*\* Rolls are triggered by the Warden, not

by you. If the Warden calls for a save or a damage roll, roll it and

report it. If you're about to describe or attempt an action and

you're not sure whether it should require a roll, \*\*stop and ask the

Warden\*\* rather than deciding for yourself or skipping the roll.



\*\*Reporting rolls:\*\* when the Warden asks for a roll, respond with

only the parameter and the result — never decide or narrate

success, failure, or consequences yourself. That's the Warden's

call.



> Example: `STR = 9, roll = 10`



\## Hit protection / harm



\*\*HP (Hit Protection) is not health.\*\* It represents stamina, luck,

and skill at avoiding a solid hit — not physical damage. It refills

on its own after a short, safe rest.



\* \*\*Armor\*\* subtracts from incoming damage before it touches HP.

\* If damage leaves HP \*\*above 0\*\*: nothing special happens — just

subtract it as normal.

\* If damage brings your HP to \*\*exactly 0\*\*: you take a \*\*Scar\*\* (a

random permanent quirk — can be a drawback, a benefit, or both)

but stay in the fight, alive.

\* If damage would take HP \*\*below 0\*\*: the leftover amount (after

HP hits 0) is subtracted directly from \*\*STR\*\* instead, and you

must immediately make a \*\*STR save\*\* using your new, lowered STR

score.



&#x20; \* \*\*Fail\*\* that save → \*\*Critical Damage\*\*: you're taken out of

the fight and will die within the hour unless treated.

&#x20; \* \*\*Succeed\*\* → battered but still conscious and fighting.

\* \*\*STR reaching 0 = death.\*\*

\* \*\*DEX reaching 0 = paralysis.\*\*

\* \*\*WIL reaching 0 = stunned / delirious.\*\*

\* Lost STR/DEX/WIL (outside the death/paralysis/delirium threshold)

recovers with about a week of rest and a healer's aid — not

quickly.

\* Other sources of attribute loss (poison, curses, ongoing

damage-over-time effects, etc.) aren't covered by this file — the

Warden adjudicates those case by case.

\* \*\*Inventory\*\*: 10 slots total (2 hands, 2 body/upper slots,

6-slot backpack). Bulky items take 2 slots.



\## House rules and do's and don'ts



\* \*\*No Exhaustion/Fatigue mechanic\*\* — this table isn't using it.

\* \*\*STR damage bonus\*\*: your STR score adds a flat bonus to every

damage roll you make:



|STR score|Damage bonus|

|-|-|

|20|+2|

|13–19|+1|

|7–12|+0|

|1–6|−1|



\* \*\*Ranged weapons\*\* get an additional DEX roll on the attack. If

that DEX roll succeeds, the attack ignores the target's armor.

\* \*\*Fate Die\*\*: any dangerous situation adds a 1d6 Fate Die

alongside the normal roll. The Warden will say a Fate Die is in

play \*before\* calling for the roll — report its result the same

way as any other roll, don't interpret it yourself.



&#x20; \* \*\*5–6\*\*: benefit on top of the normal result.

&#x20; \* \*\*3–4\*\*: plain result, no change.

&#x20; \* \*\*1–2\*\*: complication — 1 is subtracted from the roll's result,

and something extra goes wrong, independent of success/failure.



&#x20; Example — PC (STR 9) tries to break a cage lock silently with

bare hands while a guard is nearby:



&#x20; \* roll = 10, fate die = 1 → adjusted roll = 9 → succeeds, but

with a complication (e.g. PC hurts itself, −1 HP).

&#x20; \* roll = 10, fate die = 3 → fails plainly (lock doesn't break,

guard doesn't notice).

&#x20; \* roll = 10, fate die = 6 → fails, but with a benefit (e.g. PC

notices the guard dropped the keys).

&#x20; \* roll = 12, fate die = 2 → adjusted roll = 11 → fails, with a

complication (lock doesn't break, guard notices).



&#x20; As above: your job as a PC-agent is to report `STR = 9, roll = 10, fate die = 1` — the Warden decides what the complication or

benefit actually is.



\## What your PCs should know



There are two different layers of "knowledge" here, and they don't

mix:



\* \*\*Mechanical knowledge\*\* (this whole rules file, your character's

exact stats, dice results, etc.) is yours to use freely to play

correctly. Applying it is not metagaming.

\* \*\*In-character knowledge\*\* is only what your character has

personally seen, heard, been told in-fiction, or could reasonably

intuit from the scene in front of them (recognizing a familiar

trope or danger cue is fair game — that's ordinary intuition, not

metagaming). Your character should never speak or act on the

\*mechanics themselves\* — numbers, dice, rule names — only on what

they'd plausibly know or believe in-world.



&#x20; > Example: you (the agent) may reason "my STR is only 9, this

&#x20; probably fails" — but your character would never say that

&#x20; out loud. Instead: \*"I want to roll for Strength — it's only 9,

&#x20; but I'll try anyway."\* Then, in character: \*"I may be weak, but

&#x20; my will is steel!"\* — sword swings.



\* They should never reference another character's private thoughts,

secrets, or knowledge unless it was said aloud in the scene.

\* If it's genuinely unclear whether your character would know or

remember something, \*\*default to not knowing it\*\*. If that gap

matters for the current action, ask the Warden rather than

assuming either way — it's cheaper to be told "actually you'd

know that" than to leak something you shouldn't have known.
